Diode analysis: what is the <b>first step</b>?	Assume every diode is <b>cut off</b> and replace it with an <b>open circuit</b>. Then find V<sub>P</sub> − V<sub>N</sub> across each diode.	diode::method
When do you <b>reject</b> the cut-off (open-circuit) assumption for a diode?	When, with the diode open, V<sub>P</sub> − V<sub>N</sub> &gt; U<sub>D</sub>. That is a contradiction: the diode would conduct.	diode::method
Conducting-state model of a diode (constant-voltage-drop model)?	A voltage source U<sub>D</sub>, <b>+ at the anode (P)</b>.<br>Si ≈ 0.7 V, Ge ≈ 0.2–0.3 V. Ideal model: U<sub>D</sub> = 0.	diode::models
After replacing a diode with its U<sub>D</sub> source, what check confirms the choice?	The current through it must flow <b>P → N</b> (I<sub>D</sub> &gt; 0). If it comes out negative, the diode is actually off.	diode::method
Several diodes all contradict the cut-off assumption. Which do you switch on first?	The one with the <b>largest discrepancy</b> (largest V<sub>P</sub> − V<sub>N</sub>). Switch on only that one, then recompute everything.	diode::multiple
Why must you re-check the other diodes after switching one on?	Node voltages change. A diode that looked forward biased can become reverse biased (and stay off).	diode::multiple
D1 from +6 V and D2 from +2 V (anodes), cathodes joined at V<sub>O</sub>, R = 1 kΩ from V<sub>O</sub> to −6 V. Si diodes. V<sub>O</sub>?	All open: V<sub>O</sub> = −6 V; D1 sees 12 V, D2 sees 8 V → D1 first.<br>V<sub>O</sub> = 6 − 0.7 = <b>5.3 V</b>. D2: 2 − 5.3 = −3.3 V → off. I = 11.3 mA.	diode::multiple diode::example
Same circuit (D1 from +6 V, D2 from +2 V, R to −6 V), but why not switch D2 on first?	D2 on gives V<sub>O</sub> = 1.3 V, putting 4.7 V forward across D1, which contradicts D1 being off. You'd have to back up anyway.	diode::multiple diode::example
Your answer leaves an ordinary diode with a reverse voltage above its breakdown voltage U<sub>BR</sub>. Conclusion?	The analysis is probably wrong (sign error, wrong reference, wrong diode switched first). Exam circuits are rarely designed that way.	diode::breakdown
Does the breakdown sanity check apply to Zener diodes?	No. A Zener is designed to operate in reverse breakdown. The check is for ordinary diodes.	diode::breakdown diode::zener
Model of a Zener working normally in a regulator (connected in reverse)?	A voltage source U<sub>Z</sub>, <b>+ at the cathode</b>.	diode::zener diode::models
The three states of a Zener diode and their models?	Forward: U<sub>D</sub> source (like a normal diode)<br>Reverse, below U<sub>Z</sub>: open circuit<br>Reverse, reaching U<sub>Z</sub>: U<sub>Z</sub> source, + at cathode	diode::zener diode::models
How do you decide whether a reverse-connected Zener is regulating?	Assume it open. If the reverse voltage across it (V<sub>N</sub> − V<sub>P</sub>) exceeds U<sub>Z</sub>, replace it with a U<sub>Z</sub> source; then check I<sub>Z</sub> &gt; 0.	diode::zener diode::method
What current condition does a Zener need to regulate properly?	I<sub>Zmin</sub> ≤ I<sub>Z</sub> ≤ I<sub>Zmax</sub> (also check against P<sub>ZM</sub> if given).	diode::zener
V<sub>I</sub> = 12 V, R = 1 kΩ, Zener U<sub>Z</sub> = 6 V (reverse) parallel with R<sub>L</sub> = 2 kΩ. V<sub>O</sub> and I<sub>Z</sub>?	Open: V<sub>O</sub> = 12·2/3 = 8 V &gt; 6 V → regulates.<br>V<sub>O</sub> = <b>6 V</b>; I<sub>R</sub> = 6 mA, I<sub>L</sub> = 3 mA, I<sub>Z</sub> = <b>3 mA</b>.	diode::zener diode::example
Same Zener regulator (12 V, R = 1 kΩ, U<sub>Z</sub> = 6 V) but R<sub>L</sub> = 500 Ω. V<sub>O</sub>?	Open: V<sub>O</sub> = 12·0.5/1.5 = 4 V &lt; 6 V → Zener stays off. V<sub>O</sub> = <b>4 V</b>.	diode::zener diode::example
Diodes in parallel: priority rule between U<sub>D</sub> and U<sub>Z</sub>?	U<sub>D</sub> takes priority over U<sub>Z</sub>.	diode::parallel
Diodes in parallel: priority rule within the same type?	The <b>smaller</b> value wins (e.g. 5.1 V Zener beats 8.2 V Zener).	diode::parallel
Why does the smallest clamp voltage win among parallel branches?	It reaches its threshold first and pins the node at its value; every other branch then sees less than it needs and stays open.	diode::parallel
When does the parallel priority rule apply directly?	Branches across the <b>same two nodes</b>, conducting for the <b>same polarity</b>, with <b>no series resistor</b> in a branch. Otherwise use the full assume-and-check method.	diode::parallel
How does the parallel priority rule agree with the 'largest discrepancy first' rule?	All parallel branches see the same open-circuit voltage, so the smallest clamp voltage gives the largest discrepancy.	diode::parallel diode::multiple
+10 V through R = 1 kΩ to a node with a forward Si diode and a reverse 5 V Zener both to ground. V<sub>O</sub>?	D wins: V<sub>O</sub> = <b>0.7 V</b>, Zener sees 0.7 V &lt; 5 V → off. I<sub>D</sub> = 9.3 mA.	diode::parallel diode::example
+10 V, R1 = 2 kΩ, R2 = 3 kΩ divider; diode from node A (anode) to a +4 V source (cathode). V<sub>A</sub>?	Open: V<sub>A</sub> = 6 V, 6 − 4 = 2 V &gt; 0.7 → on.<br>V<sub>A</sub> = <b>4.7 V</b>; I<sub>D</sub> = 2.65 − 1.57 = 1.08 mA &gt; 0 ✓	diode::method diode::example
